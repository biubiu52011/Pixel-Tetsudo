## Executive Summary

Comparing Pixel-Tetsudo against three industry reference products (NAVITIME Japan, Google Maps Transit, Yahoo!乗換案内) reveals both strengths and significant gaps in information architecture. The core routing flow is functional but presents information in a way that diverges from established user mental models.

---

## Reference Product Analysis

### NAVITIME Japan
- **Default view:** Route result shows line names with color bars, station list collapsed, total duration prominent
- **Line identification:** Line name + line color bar + station sequence
- **Transfer visualization:** Clear transfer station highlighted with time cost
- **Direction:** Station order implies direction; no separate arrow indicator needed
- **Duration:** Total time at top, segment times in detail

### Google Maps Transit
- **Default view:** Card-based results with line color + name + duration
- **Line identification:** Color bar + line name; no OS codes shown
- **Transfer visualization:** Transfer points as nodes in a timeline
- **Direction:** Not explicitly shown; implied by station order
- **Duration:** Total time prominent; per-segment times in expandable detail

### Yahoo!乗換案内
- **Default view:** Text-heavy route with line names and station lists
- **Line identification:** Line name only; no icons or color bars
- **Transfer visualization:** Transfer station clearly marked
- **Direction:** Implicit in station sequence
- **Duration:** Total time + per-segment times

## Comparison: Pixel-Tetsudo vs Industry

### What Pixel-Tetsudo Does Well

| Aspect | Pixel-Tetsudo | Industry | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Visual line identification | Color bar + OS code badge | Color bar only | GOOD - OS codes add useful context |
| Duration prominence | 20px bold at top | 20px bold at top | GOOD |
| Station sequence | Horizontal scrollable | Vertical timeline | ACCEPTABLE |
| Transfer indication | Dedicated transfer node | Highlighted station | GOOD |
| Multiple route options | Sort mode buttons | Numbered results | QUESTIONABLE |

### What Diverges From Industry

#### D-01 🟠 WEAK - Sort Mode Buttons Instead of Numbered Results

Industry products show 3-5 pre-computed route options (fastest, fewest transfers, recommended) as numbered cards. Pixel-Tetsudo shows ONE route with 3 toggle buttons to re-compute.

**User impact:** Medium. Power users appreciate the flexibility; casual users may be confused by the toggle.

**Industry convention:** Google Maps, NAVITIME, Yahoo! all show multiple routes simultaneously. The toggle approach is unusual.

**Verdict:** QUESTIONABLE - deviates from established convention without clear user benefit.

---

#### D-02 🟡 QUESTIONABLE - Direction Arrows Are Redundant

The search result shows both:
1. A vertical station sequence (top to bottom = departure to arrival)
2. Up/down arrows on each ride segment

**Analysis:** The station sequence already implies direction. The arrows add noise without adding information.

**Industry comparison:** No major transit app shows explicit direction arrows on each segment. Direction is always implied by station order.

**Verdict:** QUESTIONABLE - redundant information increases visual complexity.

---

#### D-03 🔵 INTENTIONAL - OS Code Badges

Pixel-Tetsudo shows OS codes (JC, JA, JK) as colored badges next to line names.

**Analysis:** This is useful for regular commuters who know the OS system. For first-time tourists, it's confusing.

**Industry comparison:** No major transit app shows internal operator coding systems. Line colors and names are sufficient.

**Verdict:** CONFLICTING - useful for power users, confusing for tourists. This is a product design decision that depends on target audience.

**Recommendation:** Keep OS badges but ensure they never replace the line name as the primary identifier.

---

#### D-04 🟢 GOOD - Pixel Art Aesthetic Is Consistent

The pixel art style is applied consistently across all pages. Font (Fusion Pixel), border styles, shadows, and color palette are uniform.

**Industry comparison:** Most transit apps use clean modern design. Pixel art is a deliberate differentiator.

**Verdict:** GOOD - aesthetic is consistent and intentional.

---

#### D-05 🟠 WEAK - Nearby Spots Mixed Into Route Result

Industry products keep tourism/sightseeing as a completely separate flow. Pixel-Tetsudo injects nearby spots into the route search result card.

**Analysis:** This creates a busy result card. Users searching for a route may not want tourism information.

**User value:** Low for route-search intent. Medium for tourism intent.

**Verdict:** QUESTIONABLE - mixes two different user intents in one view.
